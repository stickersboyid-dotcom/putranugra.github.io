The Problem

In April 2021, new loyalty program registrations dropped 30% in a single month, from 4,217 to 2,916. The instinct was predictable.

Is the program attractive enough? Is the UI confusing? Are the benefits clear?

All reasonable assumptions. All wrong.

What I Did First

Before touching anything, I coordinated with the Ops & Customer Experience team to reach customers who had bought Hangry food but never joined the program. I asked them two things directly: did they know the loyalty program existed, and if yes, why hadn't they joined?

The answers were consistent:

"I know about the points, but I'm too lazy to scan. I have to download the app first."

"I don't want another app. My phone storage is already full."

"I'd like to join, but I don't really know what the benefits are. If I have to download the app just to find out, I'm not interested."

The real barrier wasn't the program. It wasn't the benefits. It wasn't even awareness. It was a single friction point: the requirement to download the Hangry App just to participate.

The Decision

Once the real problem was clear, the direction followed naturally. Instead of redirecting users to the App Store when they scanned their receipt QR code, they'd land on a web page where they could:

See the benefits → Register directly → Collect points — all without downloading anything.

One additional call worth noting: I designed this mobile-only. The trigger is scanning a receipt QR code, that happens on a phone. Designing desktop-first would've added a device-switching step that didn't need to exist.

Details flow — from QR scan to user getting points

The Pushback

Stakeholders raised a legitimate concern: "If users can collect points without the app, why would they ever download it?"

My answer was to design around that tension rather than ignore it. The landing page would always surface the app, but the app would offer more. Point redemption for merch and vouchers stayed app-only.

Landing page = entry point. App = the richer experience worth upgrading to.

Outcome

Period New Registrations Change
Feb–Apr 2021 (before) 11,624 —
May–Jul 2021 (after) 20,476 +76%
June 2021 (peak) — +124% vs May

The program that was losing users month-over-month reversed course, not because the product changed, but because the barrier to entry was removed.

What This Taught Me

"The most dangerous assumptions aren't the ones that are clearly wrong. They're the ones that are reasonable."

"The UI is confusing." "The benefits aren't attractive." Both pointed toward redesign. Both were wrong. The real problem had nothing to do with design at all. Finding it required asking the right people the right questions, not staring harder at the product.

Before deciding what to fix, make sure you understand what's actually broken.
